\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\method{} treats an attributed graph as a node-aligned, recoverable point set.
The feature baseline identifies node content, the structural deviation identifies
relation-conditioned correction, and two decoders specify what the representation
must preserve.  Point and invariant set readouts use this representation for
prediction; set expansion and pairwise relation recovery extend it to constrained
growth.  The aligned comparison table shows strong results across Web relation,
program, circuit, timing, molecular, and social graphs, while the autoregressive
study reports constrained free growth separately from representation readability
under its stated protocol.

The paper's claims remain evidence-bounded.  Direct link and feature recovery,
measured cost, and distributional generation quality require their own tables;
they cannot be inferred from accuracy or terminal validity.  This separation is
the intended contribution of \method{}: a graph representation interface whose
declared information, task utility, and growth behavior can be evaluated as
different questions (Sections~\ref{sec:recovery}--\ref{sec:ar-results}).
